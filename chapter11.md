# Chapter 11: Immunity, Inflammation, Infection, and Cancer

Because mitochondria descended from an ancient alphaproteobacterium, our cells carry a paradoxical secret: **inside every nucleated human cell live thousands of domesticated bacteria whose DNA, formylated peptides, and cardiolipin lipids still bear the molecular signatures of a prokaryotic invader**. As long as those molecules remain safely sealed behind the double mitochondrial membrane, the immune system stays quiet. The moment mitochondrial membranes are breached—or when their metabolic pathways are rewired—mitochondria transform into the central command post of **innate immunity, sterile inflammation, post-viral syndromes, and cancer**.

## The Innate Immune Command Post: MAVS, cGAS–STING, and the Inflammasome

Four major innate immune pathways are physically anchored on or triggered by mitochondria:

1. **Antiviral Defense via MAVS:** Discovered by **Zhijian "James" Chen** in 2005, **MAVS (Mitochondrial Antiviral-Signaling Protein)** is anchored into the outer mitochondrial membrane. When cytosolic RNA sensors (**RIG-I** or **MDA5**) detect viral double-stranded RNA (such as influenza, SARS-CoV-2, or hepatitis C), they translocate to the mitochondrial surface and induce MAVS to polymerize into prion-like filaments. This requires intact mitochondrial membrane potential ($\Delta \Psi_m$) and MFN1/2 fusion, recruiting TBK1 and IKK$\epsilon$ to phosphorylate **IRF3/IRF7** and launch the **Type I Interferon ($\text{IFN}-\alpha/\beta$)** antiviral defense.
2. **Cytosolic mtDNA and the cGAS–STING Pathway:** When mitochondrial stress, BAX/BAK macropores, **VDAC1 oligomers** (discovered by **Jay Chung** at the NIH in *Science* 2019), or **mPTP-driven inner membrane herniation** (discovered by **Benjamin Kile** in *Science* 2018) allow fragments of **mtDNA** to escape into the cytosol, the cytosolic DNA sensor **cGAS (cyclic GMP-AMP synthase)** binds the mtDNA and synthesizes the second messenger **$2'3'$-cGAMP**. This activates **STING** on the ER, driving a potent Type I interferon and NF-$\kappa$B inflammatory cascade.
3. **Oxidized mtDNA and the NLRP3 Inflammasome:** As **Michael Karin** and **Moshe Arditi** demonstrated, when Toll-like receptor signaling stimulates mitochondrial **CMPK2** to synthesize new, uncompacted mtDNA strands that are immediately oxidized by mitochondrial ROS into **8-oxoguanine-modified mtDNA (ox-mtDNA)**, fragments of ox-mtDNA exit through mPTP/VDAC pores and directly bind the **NLRP3 inflammasome**, activating **Caspase-1** to cleave pro-**IL-1$\beta$** and pro-**IL-18** and trigger pyroptosis.
4. **Extracellular Mitochondrial DAMPs (TLR9 and FPR1):** As trauma surgeon **Carl Hauser** (*Nature* 2010) showed, massive tissue crush injury or necrosis releases whole mitochondria, unmethylated CpG **mtDNA**, and **N-formyl peptides** into the systemic circulation. Because neutrophils mistake these ancestral bacterial molecules for a systemic bacterial infection (via **TLR9** and **Formyl Peptide Receptor 1, FPR1**), trauma patients can develop lethal sterile **Systemic Inflammatory Response Syndrome (SIRS)** without a single bacterium present.

## Immunometabolism: Succinate vs. Itaconate and T-Cell Exhaustion

Pioneered by **Luke O'Neill** (Trinity College Dublin), **Erika Pearce** (Johns Hopkins / Max Planck), **Ruslan Medzhitov** (Yale), and **Edward Pearce**, the field of **immunometabolism** has revealed that the functional identity of an immune cell—whether it attacks or repairs—is dictated directly by the wiring of its mitochondria:

### Macrophage Polarization: The Broken TCA Cycle and Itaconate
* **Pro-Inflammatory M1 Macrophages:** When a macrophage encounters bacterial lipopolysaccharide (LPS), it shuts down oxidative phosphorylation (in part via nitric oxide inhibiting Complex IV) and breaks its TCA cycle at two specific checkpoints:
  1. At **Isocitrate Dehydrogenase (IDH)**, causing **citrate** to accumulate. Part of this citrate is exported to make inflammatory prostaglandins, while another part is converted inside the matrix by the inducible enzyme **ACOD1 (IRG1)** into **itaconate**.
  2. At **Succinate Dehydrogenase (SDH)**, causing **succinate** to accumulate. Oxidation of accumulated succinate drives **Reverse Electron Transport (RET)** at Complex I, generating a burst of mitochondrial ROS that stabilizes **HIF-1$\alpha$** to transcribe pro-inflammatory **IL-1$\beta$**.
* **Itaconate as the Endogenous Anti-Inflammatory Brake:** Discovered as an immunometabolite by **Karsten Hiller** and elucidated by **Luke O'Neill** (*Nature* 2018) and **Maxim Artyomov**, **itaconate** acts as the macrophage's built-in fire extinguisher: it competitively inhibits SDH (halting RET-driven ROS) and alkylates cysteine residues on **KEAP1**, liberating the master antioxidant transcription factor **NRF2** to resolve inflammation and initiate tissue repair (**M2 alternatively activated macrophages**, which rely on intact mitochondrial OXPHOS and fatty acid $\beta$-oxidation).

### T-Cell Fate and Tumor-Induced Mitochondrial Exhaustion
As **Erika Pearce** and **Nabil Ahmed / Greg Delgoffe** demonstrated:
* **Effector T-Cells ($\text{T}_{\text{eff}}$):** Upon antigen stimulation, naive T-cells undergo **DRP1-mediated mitochondrial fission** and switch to rapid aerobic glycolysis and glutaminolysis to clone themselves exponentially.
* **Long-Lived Memory T-Cells ($\text{T}_{\text{mem}}$):** To survive for decades after an infection clears, memory T-cells require **OPA1-mediated mitochondrial fusion**, tightly packed cristae, and high **Spare Respiratory Capacity (SRC)** fueled by fatty acid oxidation.
* **T-Cell Exhaustion in Cancer:** Why do killer $\text{CD8}^+$ Tumor-Infiltrating Lymphocytes (TILs) become "exhausted" inside solid tumors? **Greg Delgoffe** and **Ping-Chih Ho** proved that inside the glucose-depleted, hypoxic tumor microenvironment, chronic antigen stimulation plus PD-1 signaling represses **PGC-1$\alpha$** and inhibits mitophagy. Exhausted T-cells accumulate swollen, depolarized, cristae-depleted mitochondria spewing ROS! Remarkably, engineering **CAR-T cells** to overexpress PGC-1$\alpha$, promoting OPA1 fusion, or treating them *ex vivo* with **Urolithin A** restores youthful mitochondrial memory phenotypes and dramatically boosts tumor eradication.

## Post-Viral Syndromes: Long COVID and ME/CFS

Why do millions of patients suffer debilitating **post-exertional malaise (PEM)**—a crushing multi-day crash in physical and cognitive energy after even minor exertion—following viral infections such as SARS-CoV-2 (**Long COVID / PASC**) or Epstein-Barr virus (**Myalgic Encephalomyelitis / Chronic Fatigue Syndrome, ME/CFS**)?

Recent clinical and molecular breakthroughs have pinpointed acquired mitochondrial dysfunction as a core biological mechanism:
1. **Viral Suppression of Mitochondrial Genes:** **Douglas Wallace** and **Keshav Singh** (*Science Translational Medicine* 2023) demonstrated that SARS-CoV-2 systematically suppresses the transcription of nuclear-encoded OXPHOS genes in the heart, kidney, liver, and lymph nodes even long after viral clearance from the lungs.
2. **ER-Stress-Induced WASF3 Disrupting Complex IV (NIH Breakthrough, 2023):** Studying skeletal muscle biopsies from patients with ME/CFS and post-infectious fatigue, **Paul Hwang** and **Avindra Nath** at the National Institutes of Health (*PNAS* 2023) discovered a striking molecular lesion: chronic endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress markedly overexpresses the actin-regulator protein **WASF3**, which translocates into mitochondria and physically binds **Complex IV (cytochrome $c$ oxidase)**, disrupting supercomplex $\text{III}_2\text{IV}$ assembly, depressing muscle oxygen consumption, and causing severe exercise intolerance—a defect that was completely reversed in preclinical models by pharmacologically reducing ER stress!
3. **Skeletal Muscle OXPHOS Crash After Exercise in Long COVID (2024):** In a landmark longitudinal muscle-biopsy study before and after maximal exercise in Long COVID patients with post-exertional malaise (**Rob Wüst** et al., *Nature Communications* 2024), researchers proved that exercise triggered an acute drop in skeletal muscle mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation capacity, accumulation of amyloid-like microdeposits in muscle tissue, and focal muscle fiber necrosis—demonstrating that post-exertional malaise has a concrete, measurable bioenergetic pathology in peripheral muscle mitochondria.

## Cancer Bioenergetics: Beyond the Warburg Effect

In the 1920s, **Otto Warburg** observed that tumor slices ferment vast amounts of glucose into lactate even in the presence of ample oxygen (**aerobic glycolysis**, or the **Warburg effect**), and he hypothesized that cancer was caused by irreversible damage to mitochondrial respiration.

Warburg's observation was right, but his explanation was wrong. As **Matthew Vander Heiden**, **Craig Thompson**, **Navdeep Chandel**, and **Ralph DeBerardinis** have demonstrated using *in vivo* stable-isotope tracing in human cancer patients, **almost all growing tumors possess active, intact mitochondria—and physically require mitochondrial respiration to grow**. When researchers delete mtDNA ($\rho^0$ cells) from aggressive tumor cells and implant them into mice, the tumor cells cannot form tumors until they **steal whole, healthy mtDNA-containing mitochondria from host stromal cells** via tunneling nanotubes (demonstrated by **Mike Berridge** in *Cell Metabolism* 2015)!

Even more sinisterly, **Shiladitya Sengupta** and colleagues (*Nature Nanotechnology* 2021) discovered that cancer cells extend **tunneling nanotubes** into attacking $\text{CD8}^+$ T-cells and literally **siphon the T-cells' mitochondria into the cancer cell**—simultaneously metabolically supercharging the tumor while paralyzing the immune cell!

Why do proliferating cancer cells need both high glycolysis *and* active mitochondria?
* **The Aspartate and Pyrimidine Requirement:** As **Vamsi Mootha** and **Matthew Vander Heiden** proved (*Cell* 2015), the single most essential job of the electron transport chain in proliferating cells is to oxidize NADH back into **$\text{NAD}^+$** so the matrix can synthesize **aspartate** (needed for purine and pyrimidine nucleotide rings) and keep ubiquinone oxidized for **DHODH** (*de novo* pyrimidine synthesis).

### Mitochondrial Oncometabolites and the Vorasidenib Breakthrough
While most tumors co-opt wild-type mitochondria, three specific classes of human cancer are directly caused by mutations in nuclear genes encoding **TCA cycle enzymes**, transforming a metabolic intermediate into an epigenetic **oncometabolite**:
1. **Isocitrate Dehydrogenase (*IDH1* and mitochondrial *IDH2*) Mutations:** Found in >70–80% of low-grade gliomas and secondary glioblastomas, ~20% of acute myeloid leukemias (AML), and cholangiocarcinomas. Instead of converting isocitrate to $\alpha$-ketoglutarate, the neomorphic mutant enzyme reduces $\alpha$-KG into the rogue oncometabolite **D-2-hydroxyglutarate (D-2-HG)**, which competitively inhibits $\alpha$-KG-dependent **TET DNA demethylases** and **JmjC histone demethylases**, hypermethylating chromatin and locking progenitor cells in an undifferentiated, cancerous state. Following the success of **enasidenib** (IDH2 inhibitor) and **ivosidenib** (IDH1 inhibitor) in leukemia, the dual IDH1/2 brain-penetrant inhibitor **vorasidenib (Voranigo)** more than doubled progression-free survival in the Phase 3 **INDIGO trial** (*NEJM* 2023) and won historic **FDA approval in August 2024** for *IDH*-mutant gliomas.
2. **Succinate Dehydrogenase (*SDHA/B/C/D*) Mutations:** Cause hereditary **paragangliomas, pheochromocytomas**, and gastrointestinal stromal tumors (GISTs) through massive **succinate** accumulation, which inhibits prolyl hydroxylases (stabilizing **HIF-1$\alpha$/HIF-2$\alpha$** pseudohypoxia) and TET/JmjC demethylases.
3. **Fumarate Hydratase (*FH*) Mutations:** Cause **Hereditary Leiomyomatosis and Renal Cell Cancer (HLRCC)** via **fumarate** accumulation, which succinates cysteines on KEAP1, suppresses homologous recombination DNA repair, and triggers release of mtDNA into the cytosol via MDVs.
